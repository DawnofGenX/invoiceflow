Narration script for the InvoiceFlow demo video (Zyrelyx Global AI Hackathon).

Every claim below is checked against a real run of the live deployment
(https://hermes-vm.tailfd1655.ts.net) recorded with scripts/record_demo.py.
Scene keys match the recorder's scene names.

Measured footage for the seven live scenes is 106s; at 133 words per minute the
script below is ~340 words (~154s of speech), so the builder pads each scene to
its narration length instead of cutting a sentence in half.


[title]      29 words
Accounts payable software decides whether to pay an invoice.
BILL, Stampli, Ramp, Melio, Rossum - none of them will tell you why.
InvoiceFlow prints the reason with every decision.


[problem]    28 words
A controller who cannot reconstruct a decision cannot sign the batch.
Audit standards ask for the reasoning, not the outcome.
So InvoiceFlow is a register, not a queue.


[register]   29 words
This is the register.
Every invoice the pipeline has seen, with its disposition:
settled, held, voided, or flagged.
Read it like a ledger, because that is what it is.


[settle]     43 words
Drop in an invoice. Four agents take it in turn.
Intake classifies it, extraction pulls the fields, validation runs five checks in order.
This one is clean, so it settles under the threshold.
And every step printed its reasoning as it went.


[record]     37 words
This is the record it wrote.
Not a status badge - a decision.
Which rule fired, how confident the agent was, and the sentence that explains it.
Served from the audit table, not reconstructed afterwards.


[duplicate]  41 words
Now the case that should never be paid twice.
The same invoice arrives again.
The duplicate rule catches it on the fourth check and closes it as flagged for review.
The system did not silently reject it, and it did not silently pay it.


[hold]       38 words
This one is different.
Every validation check passed - the document is clean.
But the vendor is new, and a new vendor never settles on its own.
The approval agent holds it for verification and prints the reason.


[held]       29 words
Here is the human queue.
Every item carries the sentence that stopped it.
Not a vague error - the actual decision, in words.


[evidence]   31 words
And the whole thing is auditable.
Every rule that has fired across the register, counted:
po match, line items, duplicate, validation, new vendor.
Alongside the automation rate and the average confidence.


[architecture] 27 words
Underneath it is one LangGraph state machine.
Each node commits its own audit rows as it goes, so a failure halfway
still leaves the completed steps visible.


[outro]      29 words
Upload an invoice.
Watch four agents reason in writing.
Read the rule that fired, the confidence, and the sentence that stopped it.
